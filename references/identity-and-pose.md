# Identity and pose system

## Identity master

Prefer a sharp, naturally lit photo with unobstructed eyes, visible jawline, and minimal beauty filtering. One photo is enough to start, but two or three angles improve difficult side views. Do not require extra photos when a single reference can support the requested pose.

Preserve:

- face shape, feature spacing, nose and lip proportions;
- glasses shape when they are part of the person's usual identity;
- hairline and recognizable hairstyle family;
- age, skin texture, and stable distinguishing marks.

Do not freeze:

- original head yaw, pitch, or roll;
- gaze direction or mouth shape;
- original clothing, crop, lighting, or background;
- the exact jaw silhouette when perspective legitimately changes it.

## Build the pose as one body

Choose the action before the face angle. Describe the pose in this order:

1. camera height and camera angle;
2. pelvis or seated orientation when visible;
3. torso direction;
4. shoulder line and weight-bearing side;
5. neck direction and visible length;
6. head yaw, pitch, and roll;
7. gaze target and expression;
8. arm and hand action.

The face must follow the skull and neck implied by the body. A three-quarter body cannot carry a pasted frontal face. If the hand points right, the torso and gaze should make that action believable rather than twisting only the wrist and neck.

Useful natural ranges for a presenter cover:

- three-quarter torso turn: about 15-35 degrees;
- head turn relative to torso: usually within 0-20 degrees;
- head tilt: usually under 8 degrees unless the action justifies more;
- camera elevation: eye level or slightly below for authority, slightly above for a friendly tutorial.

These are guides, not rigid measurements. Natural anatomy wins.

## Pose families

Select the family that best explains the topic:

- **Explain:** open palm beside a product panel; torso and eyes share the panel direction.
- **Point:** one finger identifies a specific UI control; shoulder and elbow support the line of action.
- **Compare:** body centered, hands or props separated left and right; head may favor the winning side.
- **Reveal:** presenter turns toward a result card or product; expression reacts to the evidence.
- **Warn:** closer crop, restrained hand gesture, direct gaze; avoid exaggerated fear faces.
- **Build:** seated or standing at a desk with a real interface or device; hands interact plausibly.

Do not reuse the same family for every cover.

## Clothing and scene variation

Keep clothing believable for the creator and topic. Vary silhouette and color between consecutive covers: T-shirt, overshirt, knit, hoodie, light jacket, or shirt. Avoid costumes unless the topic calls for them. Match scene light to the person; do not combine a warm face with a cool scene without a motivated rim or fill light.

## Anatomy rejection checklist

Reject and regenerate when any is true:

- neck exits the skull or collar at an implausible angle;
- jaw, ear, neck, and shoulder perspective disagree;
- face lighting or sharpness differs from the body as if composited;
- glasses fail to wrap around the new face angle;
- hand has fused, missing, or surplus fingers;
- gesture does not connect to shoulder and elbow mechanics;
- identity is recognizable only because the exact source face was pasted in.
